## Personal story

My studies in **Tourism** and **Chinese Language and Literature** have taught me to see travel as both a service system and a cultural encounter. Courses in tourism economics, revenue management, marketing, programming, and machine learning added a quantitative lens: they showed me how data can reveal why people make decisions and how organizations can respond more thoughtfully.

That intersection now guides the questions I want to pursue in graduate study. How can hospitality organizations use customer data without losing trust? How can AI improve service operations while remaining sensitive to human emotion and local culture? And how can destinations create growth that is financially viable, socially inclusive, and sustainable?

Across strategy projects, international student programs, and an internship supporting manufacturing IT implementation, I have learned to move between analysis and execution. I enjoy turning an ambiguous problem into a structured framework, bringing together different perspectives, and translating technical possibilities into decisions people can act on.

<div class="profile-quote">
  <p>My goal is to connect rigorous analysis with service design that remains attentive to people, place, and context.</p>
</div>
