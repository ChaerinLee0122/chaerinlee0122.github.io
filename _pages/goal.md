A thorough 3D analysis of the ultra-luxury Aman Group’s resorts and hotels, a study on ways to highlight the attractiveness of a large province in Korea—these are some of my consulting projects.

But they are *class* projects. Even so, I gave them my 100%, never hesitating to cold-call hoteliers, eventually having face-to-face interviews with them at their hotels (they were surprisingly welcoming, saying that it is not every day that an undergraduate student takes such initiative), and conducting many workshops for my teams.

In many ways, I was fortunate to have so many field experiences because of the unique nature of my department; we believe that the answer is in the field. It was also an excellent training ground for learning how to back up claims and recommendations with *numbers,* as over 90% of the classes are quantitative, covering not only basic statistics and economics but also PLC analysis, ERRC framework, and PEST analysis. In fact, many of my friends wondered whether my major is mathematics.

And I love these quantitative classes.

But I am still very thirsty. For one, there are all too many variables that are not accounted for. For one, hospitality and tourism are about *taste*—people's preferences for food and sightseeing, for instance—which is not easy to measure.

However, as a firm believer in the power of numbers, I do believe that it is measurable given the right questions. That is, I need to identify the countless variables that shape taste. Only when we thoroughly understand our customers and their preferences can we develop creative tourism products, for instance.

Moreover, my industries, by default, are global as we aim to cater to everyone in the world —literally speaking, which translates into the need to study Big Data.

In addition, to analyze customer data, studying the text alone won’t suffice. That is, every second as we speak, there are delighted or angry customers calling in. Yet, whereas even a complaint could be a rich source of data, most of this audio data is lost. The list goes on; as customers return from a vacation, they often leave posts on their SNS channels—all of which are once again valuable “emotional” (like or dislike) data.

Therefore, there is a need to study ways to make these data count. For this, I really must study the set of tools to collect (much valuable data already exists but is buried deep in dusty cabinets, so to speak), cleanse it (much data is messy at best, while bias is always a concern), and analyze data that comes in all different forms. Even in text, many people use in-group coded language, like a deliberate misspelling, so outsiders will not catch it.

As such, I plan to join a consulting firm afterward to help existing companies better understand their customers and provide better services. Later, if I may, I hope to start my own consulting company that caters to the key players in my industries (there is no such consultancy in Korea at this point).

And I know for a fact that there will be demand because even the five-star hotels today manage customer data 1D. Yes, we do know their name, gender, and frequency of stay, but except for a few VIPs, the data collected is often limited. Meanwhile, the demand for this type of consultancy will be significant, I am sure, as my industries are scale industries.

This, in turn, translates into my other needs; as investments are large, a single risky or wrong decision can be costly. Hence, risk management is a must, while thorough and in-depth analysis is critical for minimizing risk.

As a last note, although my target industries may seem rather clear-cut, in many ways, they are not merely about tourism and hospitality. Instead, as I study data for these industries, I also need to study countless other imaginable industries: food, health care, luxury goods, sports, and many more, as a hotel, for instance, offers all of these services. That is, my industries are a microcosm of how we live and what we need. As such, I have so much to learn about the industries that are included, or relevant to, my own.

Therefore, I need to study diverse industries and the ways to analyze different industries.

I want to help provide “good things in life” that contribute to a good life.
